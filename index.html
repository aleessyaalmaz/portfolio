Educational Mobile Game

From Snow to Ocean: Pingu's Big Green Journey

A top-down pixel-art educational game where Pingu, a green penguin, travels from the snowy Arctic to the ocean and the beach, clearing rubbish, cleaning up toxic waste and confronting fishermen to teach players about protecting the environment.

Built in Unity 6 for mobile, players move Pingu with a joystick on the left of the screen and tap the button on the right to interact with objects, completing each level objective to move on. It includes a main menu and levels set in the Arctic, ocean and beach. Made with Unity 6, C# and Aseprite.

Computer Graphics Project

SpaceCraft: Spacecraft Shooting Game

A 2D space shooter built for the Computer Graphics course. Fly a spacecraft, shoot descending enemy ships and avoid obstacles to score points, drawn entirely with OpenGL primitives.

Written in C++ with OpenGL and GLUT, developed in the Code::Blocks IDE with an MSYS2 toolchain. Players start from a welcome and tutorial screen, move with the arrow keys and shoot with the spacebar. Each enemy shot adds 10 points, obstacles block movement, and colliding with an enemy ends the game, with Enter to restart. Done as a team of four.

UI/UX Design Project (TMT2703)

Project UNIMAS E-Shop

A group UI/UX project designing a mobile online shopping app for UNIMAS students, with affordable prices, easy navigation, secure payment and quick customer support in mind.

Followed a user-centred process: interviews and an affinity diagram, four user personas, a storyboard, hand-drawn wireframes and a high-fidelity Figma prototype covering login, shopping, cart, checkout, QR/card payment, order tracking, ratings, a help centre and scam safety tips. Usability testing with 5 participants found login was easy for everyone and navigation was clear for 4 out of 5, with product labelling flagged for improvement. Done as a team of five.

AR Application (CGDD)

CGDD AR Application: World of Pathology & Anatomy

An educational augmented reality app that lets visitors scan an organ image with their phone camera and explore it as a 3D model with key facts, inspired by our visit to the Pathology & Anatomy Museum at the Faculty of Medicine and Health Sciences, UNIMAS.

Built in Unity with Vuforia image recognition and tracking. After scanning, the app loads an information screen for the organ (such as the lungs or heart) showing its location, function, main parts and fun facts beside the 3D model. I worked on the UI/UX design with a teammate, including the main menu, app description, credits, tutorial, camera and settings screens, in a team of five.
